## 1. SIMAH (Kilian et al. 2025, Lancet Public Health; Probst et al. 2023 AJE; released code): annual cycle, age range, entrants/migrants, cause-specific deaths, population size/runs, calibration and validation

### Takeaway
SIMAH is a dynamic, stochastic, individual-level microsimulation of US adults aged 18-79 (open population: new 18-year-olds and in-migrants added each year, deaths and out-migrants removed, everyone aged out after 79), stepped annually from 2000; drinking is held both as a 4-level WHO category and continuous g/day, plus an annually re-predicted binary HED flag. In the released code the yearly order is: HED update -> (policy shock) -> outputs -> deaths from non-modelled causes -> RR-based deaths from 10 alcohol-related causes -> education transitions (age <=34) -> alcohol category transitions + new g/day -> former-drinker reassignment -> age+1 -> drop >79 -> add 18-year-olds, add in-migrants, remove out-migrants. Calibration is Bayesian history matching (not IMIS, as far as could be verified) on education and drinking-category prevalence; mortality is not calibrated but rescaled to vital statistics.

### Cited Findings
Design and population (Kilian 2025 article)
- Synthetic population "representative of the real US population aged 18–79 years at the national level"; baseline year 2000, one-year steps to 2019; policy implemented in 2019 and compared with a no-policy reference — [Kilian et al. 2025, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-2667(25)00165-3)
- Individuals described by education (high school or less / some college / college degree or more, proxy for SES), race and ethnicity (non-Hispanic White, non-Hispanic Black, Hispanic, other), age and sex — [Kilian 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-2667(25)00165-3)
- Drinking outcomes: average g/day and WHO categories: abstainer (past 12 months); category 1 ≤20 g/day women / ≤40 men; category 2 >20–40 women / >40–60 men; category 3 >40 women / >60 men — [Kilian 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-2667(25)00165-3)
- "Every year, individuals are added to the population through births (aged 18 years) and inward migration and removed through mortality and outward migration." — [Kilian 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-2667(25)00165-3)
- Data: decennial Census + annual ACS (population), PSID 2005–2019 biennial (education transitions via Markov models), NVSS death records (mortality), BRFSS 2000–19 (alcohol exposure); alcohol-category transitions from "ordinal logistic regression modelling of pseudo-longitudinal BRFSS data (2000–10)" — [Kilian 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-2667(25)00165-3)
- Model documented with the ODD protocol in the appendix (pp 3–22); three global assumptions: data valid/generalisable; Markov (education) and ordinal (alcohol) structures are reasonable; policy mechanisms approximate real effects — [Kilian 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-2667(25)00165-3)

Calibration, validation, runs (Kilian 2025)
- Two-step Bayesian framework: step 1 education transition probabilities, step 2 alcohol ordinal-regression coefficients treated as priors; posteriors via "Bayesian history matching" against ACS 2000–10 (education) and BRFSS 2011–15 (alcohol category prevalence); parameter sets retained if "mean implausibility metric <3"; validation on held-out ACS 2011–19 and BRFSS 2016–19 — [Kilian 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-2667(25)00165-3)
- Uncertainty: 60 parameter combinations (calibrated education + alcohol models) paired with elasticity draws; each run 10 times with different seeds and averaged → 600 runs, 60 effect estimates per scenario; CIs = min–max across the 60 — [Kilian 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-2667(25)00165-3)

Policy-effect application order (Kilian 2025)
- (1) participation elasticity applied first, by proportionally sampling past-year drinkers to stop, weighted by their category-specific probability of transitioning to abstinence; (2) among continuing drinkers, beverage-specific own-price elasticities applied individually, with a U-shaped association between consumption and individual elasticity at correlation r = 0.60 (sensitivity r = 0.8 without participation elasticity; r = 0.4 with it); (3) beverage use assigned by sampling probability of drinking beer, wine, spirits by alcohol category and demographics; effect = 1-year policy change in 2019 — [Kilian 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-2667(25)00165-3)

Project design paper (Probst 2023 AJE)
- SIMAH aims to model "life-course changes in SES and alcohol use and cause-specific mortality attributable to alcohol use by SES, race/ethnicity, age, and sex"; "Markov models will be used to inform transition intensities between levels of SES and drinking patterns" — [Probst et al. 2023, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwad018)
- Via search extraction of the AJE paper / White Rose protocol PDF (not opened directly): synthetic population of 1,000,000 individuals (~0.6% of US adults in 2000) built by iterative proportional fitting from BRFSS, Census, ACS and PSID; baseline 1 January 2000; nine cause-of-death categories compared with observed age-standardised mortality by sex and education; model reproduced rising 2000–2018 mortality for AUD, liver disease, suicide and other unintentional injuries but not motor vehicle accidents — [Probst 2023 AJE](https://academic.oup.com/aje/article/192/5/690/7000827); [SIMAH protocol PDF (White Rose)](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/198292/1/SIMAH_protocol_technical%20review.pdf)
- Earlier SIMAH-team transition work used multistate Markov models on NESARC waves 2001–02/2004–05 (n=34,165): 1-year probability of staying in the same state 90.1% (non-drinker), 90.2% (category I), 31.8% (category II), 52.2% (category III) — [Puka et al. 2022, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/add.16024)

Released code (`charlotteprobst/simah` v1.0.1): exact annual order in `microsimulation()` — [microsimulation.R](https://github.com/charlotteprobst/simah/blob/2fd8c1ea8c95de0a7137e31db2560a5ceb60339d/R/microsimulation.R)
- Loop `for (y in 2000:maxyear)`: (1) `update_hed()` (skipped in the TMREL counterfactual, which sets g/day=0, drinker/former-drinker/HED = FALSE); (2) if `y %in% year_policy`: `apply_basic_policy()` then `update_alcohol_cat()`; (3) alcohol/HED summaries and population snapshot saved; (4) `apply_death_counts()` = deaths from causes NOT modelled explicitly ("REST"); (5) `assign_rr()` + join of TMREL base rates for year y + `simulate_mortality()` + `summary_disease()` + `remove_individuals()` for each modelled cause; (6) `education_update()` for age ≤34 only; (7) `transition_alcohol()` → `allocate_gramsperday_sampled()` → `drinkingstatus` recomputed → `update_former_drinker()`; (8) `age <- age + 1`; (9) `subset(data, age <= 79)`; (10) if `y <= 2030`: `add_new_18yo()`, `add_new_migrants()`, `remove_emigrants()` — [microsimulation.R](https://github.com/charlotteprobst/simah/blob/2fd8c1ea8c95de0a7137e31db2560a5ceb60339d/R/microsimulation.R)
- Default explicitly modelled causes: AUD, DM, HLVDC (hepatitis-pathway liver cirrhosis), HYPHD, IHD, IJ (intentional injury), ISTR, LVDC (liver cirrhosis main pathway), MVACC, UIJ (other unintentional injury) — 10 causes — [microsimulation.R](https://github.com/charlotteprobst/simah/blob/2fd8c1ea8c95de0a7137e31db2560a5ceb60339d/R/microsimulation.R)
- Non-modelled causes: subgroup death counts (cells = sex × 5-year age band 18–24…75–79 × race × education) divided by the current simulated cell size give stacked cause probabilities; each person gets one uniform draw; those drawn into a non-modelled cause are removed; YLL_REST = 75 − age if age < 75 — [apply_death_counts.R](https://github.com/charlotteprobst/simah/blob/2fd8c1ea8c95de0a7137e31db2560a5ceb60339d/R/apply_death_counts.R)
- Modelled causes: individual risk_c = RR_c(g/day, HED, former-drinker, sex) × rate_c (subgroup mortality rate at the theoretical minimum risk exposure level, by sex × 10-year age band × race × education × year); risks are stacked across causes, renormalised if any cumulative risk >1, and a single uniform draw allocates death to at most one cause; lifetime abstainers get zero AUD risk; YLL = 75 − age if <75 — [simulate_mortality.R](https://github.com/charlotteprobst/simah/blob/2fd8c1ea8c95de0a7137e31db2560a5ceb60339d/R/simulate_mortality.R)
- Variance reduction: staged deaths are "inflated" (default `inflation_factors = c(28, 3)` for ages 18–64 and 65–79); the number actually removed per cell is n_staged / factor, chosen by probability-proportional-to-size sampling without replacement on the individual RR (`ppswor`); reported deaths/YLL are divided by the same factor — [remove_individuals.R](https://github.com/charlotteprobst/simah/blob/2fd8c1ea8c95de0a7137e31db2560a5ceb60339d/R/remove_individuals.R); [summary_disease.R](https://github.com/charlotteprobst/simah/blob/2fd8c1ea8c95de0a7137e31db2560a5ceb60339d/R/summary_disease.R)
- RR functional forms (examples): AUD log-linear in g/day, capped at 122.51 (men) / 114.12 (women) g/day, separate former-drinker RR; LVDC log-quadratic capped at 179.44 / 94.15 g/day; HLVDC log-quadratic capped 146.3; IJ log-linear capped 100 / 50; MVACC log-linear with an additive HED term for <60 g/day, ≥60 g/day always carries the HED term, cap 150; IHD categorical bands <1.3, 1.3–25, 25–45, 45–65, ≥65 g/day (abstainer RR = 1); DM restricted cubic spline for women, RR capped at 30 — [assign_rr_*.R](https://github.com/charlotteprobst/simah/tree/2fd8c1ea8c95de0a7137e31db2560a5ceb60339d/R)
- Bundled parameter set (one sample; `inst/extdata/risk_param.rds`): e.g. former-drinker log-RRs AUD men 0.560 / women 1.218; LVDC 0.728; IHD men 0.379 / women 0.142; IJ men 0.277 / women 0.481; MVACC, UIJ, ISTR former-drinker log-RR = 0; HED log-RR term MVACC 1.050, UIJ 0.615 — [inst/extdata](https://github.com/charlotteprobst/simah/tree/2fd8c1ea8c95de0a7137e31db2560a5ceb60339d/inst/extdata)
- Alcohol transitions: ordinal logistic model on lagged category (non-drinker/low/medium/high), sex, age group (18–24, 25–64, 65+), education, race, and education × lagged-category interactions; new category drawn by one uniform draw on cumulative probabilities — [transition_alcohol.R](https://github.com/charlotteprobst/simah/blob/2fd8c1ea8c95de0a7137e31db2560a5ceb60339d/R/transition_alcohol.R)
- Continuous g/day: only those who changed to a drinking category get a new value, drawn from a beta distribution fitted to BRFSS by category × education × age group × race × sex (192 groups bundled), assigned rank-preservingly (old order kept), capped at 200 g/day; non-drinkers set to 0 — [allocate_gramsperday_sampled.R](https://github.com/charlotteprobst/simah/blob/2fd8c1ea8c95de0a7137e31db2560a5ceb60339d/R/allocate_gramsperday_sampled.R)
- Former drinkers: "not tracked over time"; each year abstainers are re-assigned former-drinker status by a uniform draw against the current proportion of former drinkers among abstainers by age category × sex — [update_former_drinker.R](https://github.com/charlotteprobst/simah/blob/2fd8c1ea8c95de0a7137e31db2560a5ceb60339d/R/update_former_drinker.R)
- HED: re-predicted every year with three XGBoost classifiers (men <35, men ≥35, women) from g/day, age, sex, race, education, threshold 0.5, for drinkers with 1 to <60 g/day; <1 g/day → no HED; ≥60 g/day → HED — [update_hed.R](https://github.com/charlotteprobst/simah/blob/2fd8c1ea8c95de0a7137e31db2560a5ceb60339d/R/update_hed.R)
- Entrants: new 18-year-olds = age-18 entry rate × current population by race × sex; donors sampled with replacement from survey microdata (years y−1 to y+1) matching sex × age 18 × race, carrying their own education and drinking — [add_new_18yo.R](https://github.com/charlotteprobst/simah/blob/2fd8c1ea8c95de0a7137e31db2560a5ceb60339d/R/add_new_18yo.R)
- In-migrants: in-migration rate × current population by age band × race × sex; donors from survey data up to 2022, and from the existing synthetic population after 2022; out-migrants: rate × cell size, capped at the cell size, sampled without replacement — [add_new_migrants.R](https://github.com/charlotteprobst/simah/blob/2fd8c1ea8c95de0a7137e31db2560a5ceb60339d/R/add_new_migrants.R); [remove_emigrants.R](https://github.com/charlotteprobst/simah/blob/2fd8c1ea8c95de0a7137e31db2560a5ceb60339d/R/remove_emigrants.R)
- Released policy module is generic: `apply_basic_policy()` draws an individual % change from `faux::rnorm_pre(log(alc_gpd)^2, mu = elasticity, sd = SE, r = r_sim_obs)` (defaults −0.1078, SE 0.0442, r = 0.8) and applies it to g/day of current drinkers; no beverage-specific or participation step, and no screening/BI module, was found in v1.0.1 (grep for "asbi|brief|screen" returned nothing) — [apply_basic_policy.R](https://github.com/charlotteprobst/simah/blob/2fd8c1ea8c95de0a7137e31db2560a5ceb60339d/R/apply_basic_policy.R)
- Bundled input years: `mort_data`, `base_rates` and `migration_rates` all span 2000–2030 — [inst/extdata](https://github.com/charlotteprobst/simah/tree/2fd8c1ea8c95de0a7137e31db2560a5ceb60339d/inst/extdata)
- The baseline synthetic population (`data.rds`) and survey donor pool (`svy_data.rds`) are not in the repository; the README downloads them from figshare — [README](https://github.com/charlotteprobst/simah/blob/2fd8c1ea8c95de0a7137e31db2560a5ceb60339d/README.md)

### Inferences
- In the code, mortality is applied to the start-of-year population before drinking transitions and ageing, so deaths in year y depend on drinking at the start of y (no lag structure, no cumulative exposure). For Chile this implies that latency-heavy causes (cancers) are poorly represented — consistent with Lemp 2026 excluding cancers.
- The bundled TMREL base rates look pre-multiplied by the inflation factor (e.g. rate_MVACC 0.0042/year for women 18–24 Black College in 2000, ≈28 × a plausible true rate); the bundled `mort_data` totals 3,339 deaths at ages 18–79 in 2000, i.e. counts already scaled to the synthetic population. Both are inferences from the data files, not documented statements.
- DESCRIPTION imports `lhs` (Latin hypercube sampling), suggesting LHS of prior parameter space before history matching; not documented in the files I read.
- Release v1.0.1 appears to be a cleaned "core" package (demography, education, alcohol, HED, mortality, generic price shock); the beverage-specific price module of Kilian 2025 and the ASBI module of Lemp 2026 seem not to be included.

### Gaps
- Kilian 2025 appendix (ODD, pp 3–22; pp 19–21 elasticity sampling; pp 24–26 CIs) not opened; synthetic population size in Kilian 2025 not stated in the main text. The "1,000,000 individuals (~0.6%)" figure comes only from search extraction of the 2023 design paper/protocol.
- No source found stating that SIMAH used IMIS. Kilian 2025 and Lemp 2026 describe Bayesian history matching / a Bayesian probabilistic framework. Whether IMIS was used at another stage (e.g. 2023 design paper) could not be verified.
- Zenodo 0.1.1 contents vs GitHub v1.0.1 not verified.

## 2. Lemp et al. 2026, JAMA Health Forum (SIMAH applied to screening and brief intervention): structural changes relative to Kilian 2025

### Takeaway
Lemp 2026 keeps the SIMAH core (18–79 synthetic US population, education + ordinal drinking transitions, history-matching calibration) but extends it to 2000–2030 with Census-based projections, adds annual HED updating, adds an RR-based cause-specific mortality/YLL module (5 key causes, HCV cirrhosis pathway), and adds an empirically parameterised care cascade (health-care access → screening → BI for hazardous drinkers) from NSDUH; BI effects (g/day reduction scaled to current use; HED removal by risk difference) apply only to individuals newly reached by expansion.

### Cited Findings
- Model: dynamic individual-level microsimulation "calibrated, and validated to simulate alcohol use and deaths due to alcohol-related causes over 30 years from 2000 through 2030, progressing in 1-year time steps"; synthetic US population aged 18 to 79; education can change until age 34; ODD protocol in eMethods 1; CHEERS reporting — [Lemp et al. 2026, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamahealthforum.2026.2348)
- Outcomes: prevalence of hazardous use (>20 g/day women, >40 g/day men; this "thereby defin[es] the population eligible for BI in the model") and YLL per 100 000 before age 75 from AUD (incl. poisonings), liver disease and cirrhosis (incl. HCV-related cirrhosis), motor vehicle injuries, other unintentional injuries and suicide (each AAF ≥15% in the US); HED (≥60 g on ≥1 occasion in past 30 days) "updated annually and modifies the cause-specific risk functions for injuries" — [Lemp 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamahealthforum.2026.2348)
- BRFSS drinking "adjusted for underreporting"; RR functions from meta-analyses; two RR functions within liver disease: chronic liver disease/cirrhosis and an attenuated function for HCV-related cirrhosis — [Lemp 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamahealthforum.2026.2348)
- Projections: population growth and birth rates from 2022 from US Census Bureau projections; migration projected by year, sex, age, race/ethnicity from 2022; mortality from 2024 projected with Census death projections; COVID: education transitions 2020–2022 parameterised with PSID 2019–2021; the same ordinal alcohol model used in all years — [Lemp 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamahealthforum.2026.2348)
- Care cascade (routine health-care access, screening, BI for hazardous drinkers) from regression models on NSDUH 2013–2019 — [Lemp 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamahealthforum.2026.2348)
- Calibration: ACS 2000–2010 (education) and BRFSS 2000–2015 (alcohol); validation ACS 2011–2019, BRFSS 2016–2019; "Mortality risks are not calibrated; instead, reference mortality rates are adjusted to align mortality outcomes from the model with the US National Vital Statistics System data and US Census Bureau projections" — [Lemp 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamahealthforum.2026.2348)
- Runs: 70 parameter sets × 20 stochastic replications = 1400 runs per scenario; 95% CrI = 2.5th–97.5th percentile across the 70 parameter sets — [Lemp 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamahealthforum.2026.2348)
- Intervention mechanics: those who would have received services under historical rates are sampled first with the empirical models; additional recipients are then selected among the remaining individuals with the same model; "Only individuals newly receiving BI due to expanded ASBI were assigned intervention effects because the effects of existing interventions are assumed to be already captured in historical alcohol use data"; g/day reduction sampled from the Cochrane trial-level distribution, mean (SE) −2.86 (0.58) g/day, "scaled using each individual's current level of consumption"; among newly intervened HED drinkers a proportion equal to the pooled RD −0.07 (95% CI −0.02 to −0.12) randomly transition to non-HED; GPD and HED changes sampled independently; sampling each year independent of the previous year, so people can be re-intervened — [Lemp 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamahealthforum.2026.2348)
- Scenarios from 2025: (1) +20 million screenings/year (mean screened proportion 55.7% → 66.9%); (2) +20 M screenings and +4 M BI; (3) +20 M and +8 M BI; (4) universal ASBI among people accessing care. Sensitivity: BI effect −1.71 or −4.01 g/day; extending eligibility to category I; start delayed to 2026–2028 — [Lemp 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamahealthforum.2026.2348)
- Reference: by 2030, 18.0% of hazardous-drinking men and 12.2% of women receive a BI annually; 2030 YLL reductions vs reference, men: −8.0 (S1), −29.0 (S2), −51.3 (S3), −68.9 (S4) per 100 000; women: −1.9, −15.1, −34.1, −52.0 — [Lemp 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamahealthforum.2026.2348)
- Stated limits: cancers excluded because of latency ("cannot be adequately represented within the present modeling framework based on annual drinking transitions"); homogeneous BI effect scaled only by baseline consumption; no referral to treatment/pharmacotherapy; NSDUH self-reported "advice to cut down" used as BI proxy — [Lemp 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamahealthforum.2026.2348)

### Inferences
- Structural deltas vs Kilian 2025: horizon 2000–2019 → 2000–2030 with exogenous projections; mortality/YLL module switched on (Kilian reported only consumption); HED as a dynamic state; care-cascade module replaces price-elasticity module; more parameter sets/replicates (60×10 → 70×20); CrI definition changed (min–max → 2.5–97.5 percentiles); alcohol calibration window stated as BRFSS 2000–2015 (Lemp) vs 2011–15 targets (Kilian) — the two descriptions may refer to the same targets with different wording; not verifiable without the appendices.
- No explicit decay/rebound of the BI effect is described in the main text: the reduced g/day simply enters subsequent ordinal transitions. Contrast SAPM (linear rebound over 7 years) and OECD SPHeP-NCDs (effect for 5 years) — see §3 and §4.
- The "only newly intervened get effects" rule is directly relevant to Chile: if baseline drinking data already embed current BI coverage (e.g. EMPA/APS programmes), only incremental coverage should carry an effect.

### Gaps
- eMethods (sections 1.1.2, 1.3.4–1.3.6, eTable 6 RR parameters) not opened: exact NSDUH cascade models, how the GPD reduction is "scaled", whether the effect persists or decays, and the underreporting adjustment are unverified.

## 3. Sheffield Alcohol Policy Model (SAPM) and its successor STAPM: structure, "deterministic" label, handling of time (lags, full effect), age range and population

### Takeaway
SAPM (v2.x–v4) combines an individual-level econometric component (survey respondents with weights; price → consumption) with a deterministic, subgroup/cohort-level epidemiological component (consumption → harms via Gunning-Schepers potential impact fractions, condition-specific time lags reaching "full effect" at 10 years in v2.6 and 20 years in v4). No random draws generate outcomes, which is why the authors call it a "causal, deterministic, epidemiological" model. The newer STAPM is a genuinely dynamic stochastic microsimulation (200,000 synthetic individuals, annual cycles, simulated deaths removed), with individuals ageing along subgroup age patterns; STAPM age coverage runs to 89.

### Cited Findings
- SAPM v2.0 mathematical description: baseline purchasing and consumption by age and gender subgroups; policy effects via price elasticities; risk functions for 47 health conditions plus crime, absenteeism, unemployment; adaptable to other countries — [Brennan et al. 2015 Health Econ, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/hec.3105)
- Framework steps: subgroup definition, harms/outcomes, modifiable risk components, baseline policy variables (prices), elasticities, risk functions for 47 conditions — [Brennan et al. 2013 Ann Oper Res, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10479-013-1451-z)
- SAPM v2.6 described as "a causal, deterministic, epidemiological model"; consumption data from 2009 General Lifestyle Survey for adults older than 16 (n=10,588); 54 subgroups by age, sex, consumption level; elasticities from a pseudo-panel of LCF 2001–2009 (10×10 own/cross matrix); peak-day consumption modelled by regression on mean consumption, age and sex; 47 conditions; PIF method adjusts observed mortality/morbidity; "We use a linear time lag function of 10 years building up to the full effect" — [Holmes et al. 2014 Lancet, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(13)62417-4)
- SAPM v2.5: population "Adults and young people aged 16 or more" — [Brennan et al. 2014 BMJ, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.g5452)
- SAPM v4: "a causal deterministic, epidemiological simulation tool"; "an individual-based econometric component that estimates how price changes affect individual-level alcohol consumption, and a cohort-based dynamic epidemiological component" for 45 conditions; adults 18+; 120 subgroups (sex × age 18–24/25–34/35–54/55+ × IMD quintile × consumption group); "full effect" at 20 years post-implementation; authors argue a full probabilistic analysis "would be at best uninformative and at worst potentially misleading" because correlation among parameter errors is unknown, and use scenario analysis instead — [Meier et al. 2021 Addiction (gender), DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/add.15464)
- SBI in SAPM (Italy adaptation): 10-year programme, 30-year horizon; subgroups by age (16–17, 18–24, 25–34, 35–44, 45–54, 55–64, 75+ as printed), sex and baseline consumption; each individual (= survey respondent) screened at most once at first opportunity via an age/sex "arrival profile"; AUDIT-C ≥5 men / ≥4 women; 10-minute BI; effect 12.3% consumption reduction (Kaner Cochrane) "assumed to rebound linearly towards the patient's baseline consumption over a period of 7 years"; 42 conditions; PIF-adjusted life tables — [Angus et al. 2014 BMC Fam Pract, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2296-15-26)
- Condition-specific lag vectors used "in the current version of SAPM" (Holmes 2012 review), 20 years: acute = 100% in year 1; cancers 0 for years 1–10 then 10/year in years 11–20; CVD (IHD, strokes) front-loaded over 10 years (30.9, 21.6, 15.1…); liver cirrhosis/pancreatitis geometric decline over 20 years (20.2, 16.2, 13.0…); alcoholic liver disease (20.7, 13.2, 9.2…) — [tobalcepi AlcLags.R](https://github.com/stapm-platform/tobalcepi/blob/054ca37190ad901aa5028bd6222e831083472eaa/R/AlcLags.R)
- STAPM: "a dynamic microsimulation modelling tool"; differs from SAPM in being dynamic ("allows the characteristics and behaviours of modelled individuals to change over time") and in covering tobacco too; "as modelled individuals age, their alcohol consumption follows the age patterns for their population subgroups" (acknowledged to conflate age and cohort trends); annual cycles; synthetic population of 200,000 individuals from Scottish Health Survey 2016–2018; 800 subgroups (age 18–24/25–34/35–49/50+, sex, income quintile, 5 smoking groups, 4 drinker groups); participation and conditional-consumption elasticities; 45 conditions; "assigns modelled individuals a relative risk ... based on their current alcohol consumption and lagged effects of consumption in previous years", applies PIFs, then "uses probabilities of death for each individual ... to simulate who dies in each year and removes those who do"; initialised 2017, run to 2040 — [Holmes et al. 2026 PLoS Med, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1004792)
- STAPM code bands age into 11–15, 16–17, 18–24, 25–34, 35–44, 45–54, 55–64, 65–74, 75–89 for single-occasion drinking parameters — [tobalcepi AlcBinge_stapm.R](https://github.com/stapm-platform/tobalcepi/blob/054ca37190ad901aa5028bd6222e831083472eaa/R/AlcBinge_stapm.R)
- Via search extraction of the STAPM site: the Sheffield Tobacco Policy Model indexes individuals by single year of age "from a possible minimum of 11 years to 89 years"; each annual step adds a new cohort at the youngest age; order within a year: mortality, then behaviour change, then ageing — [STPM page](https://stapm.gitlab.io/STPM.html)
- France SAPM adaptation (cancers, 2014–2050): for each gender-age group, 1000 consumption values drawn from calibrated group-specific gamma distributions; PIF by cancer site — [Ren et al. 2021 Addiction, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/add.15426)

### Inferences
- "Deterministic individual-based" (the phrase in the brief) is a fair paraphrase: individual respondents carry consumption and price responses, but harm is computed deterministically at subgroup level through PIFs on a cohort that ages; the exact phrase "deterministic individual-based" was not found in the sources read.
- SAPM's handling of time is "policy shock + lagged convergence to full effect" (10 or 20 years), i.e. comparative statics plus lag functions rather than simulated drinking trajectories; STAPM adds trajectories but only via subgroup age patterns, not estimated individual transitions.
- For Chile: the Sheffield lag vectors are a ready, citable default for an annual-cycle PIF-to-mortality bridge; SIMAH has no lags.

### Gaps
- The lag code's roxygen says it returns cumulative proportions but the `cumsum` line is commented out with a "QA check [error]" note; whether the vectors are annual increments or cumulative in production could not be confirmed.
- SAPM v4 technical appendix (open access) not opened; exact upper age in SAPM v4 health module not verified (STAPM: 89).

## 4. OECD SPHeP-NCDs (OECD 2021 "Preventing Harmful Alcohol Use"): Chile inclusion, alcohol exposure, policies incl. brief interventions, ages, mortality link

### Takeaway
SPHeP-NCDs is a dynamic, stochastic, lifetime (birth-to-death) microsimulation with demographic, risk-factor and disease modules; individuals keep a fixed risk-factor quantile, relative risks come from GBD, and disease incidence/fatality/remission generate deaths and life expectancy. The 2021 alcohol report covers 52 OECD/EU27/G20 countries (Chile is an OECD member, so very likely included, but no Chile-specific output was verified). Brief interventions were modelled as counselling in primary care with modest yearly reach and a 5-year effect (search extraction; not verified against the PDF).

### Cited Findings
- Model description: "a dynamic microsimulation model" with "three core modules – a demographic, a risk factor and a disease module"; demographic module assigns birth date, gender and migration status to create "synthetic life histories (i.e. from birth to death)"; "individuals are permanently allocated to a fixed quantile for each risk factor"; RRs "based on those outlined in Global Burden of Disease study"; disease module simulates incidence, fatality and remission; outputs include life expectancy, prevalence, DALYs, costs; policy inputs = target population, exposure, individual effectiveness, time to maximum effect and effect over time — [Cheatley et al. 2021 Mol Oncol (SPHeP-NCDs), DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/1878-0261.12812)
- In that 2020–2050 cancer analysis (37 countries) Mexico is included and Chile is not listed; 10% alcohol price increase input: consumption −4% to −7%; MUP: −0.6% to −3.3% — [Cheatley 2021](https://doi.org/10.1002/1878-0261.12812)
- Via search extraction of the SPHeP-NCDs documentation: alcohol tracked as g/day plus pattern (regular, binge, AUD); moderate <40 g men / <20 g women, heavy up to 60 / 40 g, harmful above; binge = proportion of drinkers with a ≥60 g (men) or ≥48 g (women) occasion in the past 30 days (IHME definition); inputs from IHME and WHO GISAH; AUD modelled as a drinking pattern, not a disease; individuals enter at birth and exit at death — [SPHeP-NCDs alcohol documentation](http://oecdpublichealthexplorer.org/ncd-doc/risk_factors/alcohol.html)
- Via search extraction of the OECD 2021 report: 52 countries (OECD, EU27, G20); life expectancy 0.9 years lower over the next 30 years on average due to drinking above 1 drink/day (women) / 1.5 (men); country note for Mexico: 1.0 year lower — [OECD 2021 report](https://doi.org/10.1787/6e4b4ffb-en); [Mexico country note](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2025/07/preventing-harmful-alcohol-use-country-notes_88239678/mexico_17a3f452/4725d453-en.pdf)
- Via search extraction of the OECD 2021 report (section 7.2.4): ~5% of primary-care patients screened, increased to 10% for modelling; about half screen positive and get BI (≈1–3% of population); in SPHeP-NCDs "each year 20% of non-alcohol-dependent heavy drinkers" benefit (≈2% of population); effectiveness from Kaner et al. 2018 Cochrane; effect lasts five years, declining linearly in the final year — [OECD 2021 report PDF](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2021/05/preventing-harmful-alcohol-use_0d828f91/6e4b4ffb-en.pdf)
- Kaner 2018 Cochrane primary meta-analysis: mean difference −20 g/week (95% CI −28 to −12) at one year — [Cochrane CD004148.pub4](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD004148.pub4/full)
- Predecessor OECD/WHO CDP-Alcohol microsimulation (2015): three settings — Canada, Czech Republic, Germany; brief interventions in primary care and tax increases "have the potential to generate large health gains" — [Cecchini, Devaux, Sassi 2015, OECD Health WP 80, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1787/5js1qwkvx36d-en)

### Inferences
- Fixed-quantile risk factors mean SPHeP-NCDs has no individual drinking transitions; changes come from shifting the distribution over calendar time/age plus policy shocks. This is the opposite design choice to SIMAH (state transitions) and a cheap option when longitudinal drinking data are lacking (Chile's ENPG is repeated cross-sectional).
- Chile's inclusion in the 52-country set follows from its OECD membership (member since 2010), but I found no Chile-specific output.

### Gaps
- OECD report pages and SPHeP-NCDs documentation were blocked; age range of the alcohol module, exact BI effect size used (g/week or %), and Chile results are unverified. The BI parameters above come from search-engine extraction of the PDF and should be checked against the report before use.

## 5. Alcohol microsimulation/policy models in Latin America, other middle-income settings, and other national models simulating individual trajectories

### Takeaway
No peer-reviewed individual-level alcohol microsimulation linking drinking trajectories to cause-specific mortality was found for Chile, Mexico, Brazil, Argentina or Colombia; regional work is macro (PIF/attributable fractions), static tax microsimulations, or OECD/World Bank multi-country tools. Among middle-income settings, the closest structural analogue is the Sheffield-team South Africa MUP model (subgroup epidemiological model, ages 15+). Individual-trajectory alcohol models remain concentrated in the US (SIMAH, CASCADE/CASCADEPOP) and UK (STAPM).

### Cited Findings
Latin America / middle-income
- Colombia: World Bank ex-ante evaluation of health taxes using a static microsimulation fed by the 2019 national psychoactive-substance survey, covering tobacco, alcohol, sugary drinks and ultra-processed food; mortality estimate (33,608 deaths/year) combines three risk factors (via search extraction) — [World Bank document](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099082025165020442/pdf/P501401-510179a1-4f3f-4b68-9db4-7fd73542558a.pdf)
- Colombia: macrosimulation (Prevent 3.01) of potential impact fractions for alcohol, tobacco, BMI and physical activity on colorectal cancer 2016–2050 — [de Vries et al. 2020 BMJ Open, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2020-037388)
- Mexico: CIEP alcohol-tax simulator (version 3.0) with policy evidence summary; deaths-averted claim (>16,000 over 10 years) whose generating model is unclear (via search extraction) — [CIEP](https://ciep.mx/evidencia-sobre-impuestos-y-politicas-publicas-sobre-alcohol-en-mexico/)
- Chile: press-reported estimate attributed to P. Margozzini that a 20% reduction in mean consumption would avoid 1,380 deaths/year; methodology not stated — [La Tercera](https://www.latercera.com/que-pasa/noticia/puede-un-alza-de-impuesto-al-alcohol-disminuir-su-consumo-el-10-de-las-muertes-en-el-pais-se-relaciona-con-su-ingesta/480583/)
- South Africa (Sheffield-team "epidemiological policy appraisal model"): population aged 15+ in 2018 stratified by drinker group (moderate, occasional binge, heavy) and wealth quintile; 20-year horizon; 5 conditions (HIV, intentional injury, road injury, liver cirrhosis, breast cancer); R10/standard-drink MUP → −4.40% consumption, 20,585 fewer deaths over 20 years — [Gibbs et al. 2021 BMJ Open, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2021-052879); equity extension (22,600 deaths averted, 56% in bottom two quintiles) — [Gibbs et al. 2022 BMJ Glob Health, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjgh-2021-007824)
- Cameroon: welfare/household microsimulation of alcohol and tobacco taxes (not individual health trajectories) — [Heliyon 2024, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2024.e40174); [Nicotine Tob Res 2026, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/ntr/ntaf254)
- India: decision tree + Markov model of alcohol-attributable liver disease, cancers and road injuries, 2011–2050 — [Jyani et al. 2019, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.drugpo.2019.04.005)
- WHO European Region (incl. Russia): macro model of a minimum tax share; 40,033 deaths averted/year (25% share) — [Neufeld et al. 2022, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lanepe.2022.100325)

Other national models
- CASCADEPOP (US, Sheffield/NIAAA): ages 12–80; base population 1 Jan 1980 built by IPF on NSDUH 1979 (n=6,105) with 138 census constraints; new individuals enter at age 12 (not birth); migration from ACS; deaths removed by drawing the total count by age category × sex with Monte Carlo sampling; on 1 January everyone ages +1; social-role transitions from msm Markov models on PSID; tested at 10% scale; R for synthesis, Repast HPC (C++) for agent-based runs; alcohol held as drinking status, g/day, frequency and HED days; demographic validation within 1.5% of Census over 30 years — [Brennan et al. 2020 Int J Microsimul, DOI](https://doi.org/10.34196/ijm.00217)
- CASCADE agent-based models (norms theory) initialised from a 1979 microsynthesis — [Probst et al. 2020 Health Educ Behav, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/1090198119880545); integrated dual-process model of US consumption 1984–2012 — [Buckley et al. 2021 Addict Behav, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.addbeh.2021.107094)
- Canada: InterMAHP is an aggregate alcohol-attributable-fraction estimator and policy scenario tool (not individual-level) — [Sherk et al. 2020 JSAD (PubMed 32527386)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32527386/); MUP and warning-label model for cancer — [Sherk et al. 2026 Lancet Public Health, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-2667(26)00006-X)
- Germany: macro PIF modelling of tax increases of 20/50/100% — [Kilian et al. 2022, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00103-022-03528-9); qualified withdrawal treatment in Bremen — [Manthey et al. 2020, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13011-019-0249-7)
- Netherlands (RIVM): three-stage model (logistic any-drinking → negative binomial weekly drinks → logistic HED), calibrated to excessive/HED prevalence 2014–2022 — [ten Dam et al. 2025 PLoS One, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0327264)
- Australia: econometric + epidemiological simulation of volumetric tax and MUP on social harms — [Marzan et al. 2024 Int J Drug Policy (PubMed 38943908)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38943908/); SimDrink agent-based model of night-time economy — [Int J Drug Policy 2016, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.drugpo.2016.02.016); Policy1-Liver model of liver disease — [Public Health 2025, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.puhe.2025.03.030)
- US liver-disease microsimulation of COVID-era drinking increases — [Hepatology 2022, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/hep.32272)

### Inferences
- A Chilean model linking ENPG-based synthetic population → transitions → BI → RR → cause-specific deaths would be, as far as these searches show, the first individual-level alcohol mortality microsimulation in Latin America.
- For middle-income adaptation the South Africa model shows a pragmatic choice: few conditions (5), subgroup-level PIFs, 15+ population, 20-year horizon.

### Gaps
- Searches in Spanish/Portuguese surfaced no peer-reviewed alcohol microsimulation for Brazil or Argentina; PAHO/WHO investment-case reports were not reviewed. No individual-level Russian alcohol microsimulation was found.

## 6. Older adults (65+) and open populations: age caps and sourcing drinking at older ages when surveys stop at 64/65

### Takeaway
Models cap or bound age differently: SIMAH 18–79 (people aged out at 80), CASCADEPOP 12–80, STAPM up to 89, SAPM open-ended adult bands (55+/75+), SPHeP-NCDs lifetime to death. All of these use exposure surveys without a 64/65 ceiling (BRFSS, NSDUH, HSE/SHeS, IHME), so none documents a method for imputing drinking above a survey age cap. Chile's ENPG covers 12–65, so a Chilean model needs an explicit, documented strategy for 66+.

### Cited Findings
- SIMAH: age +1 then "remove anyone over 79"; mortality bands 65–74 and 75–79; alcohol transition model pools 65+ into one age group; the mortality inflation factor for 65–79 is 3 vs 28 for 18–64 (older ages have enough deaths) — [microsimulation.R](https://github.com/charlotteprobst/simah/blob/2fd8c1ea8c95de0a7137e31db2560a5ceb60339d/R/microsimulation.R); [transition_alcohol.R](https://github.com/charlotteprobst/simah/blob/2fd8c1ea8c95de0a7137e31db2560a5ceb60339d/R/transition_alcohol.R)
- Lemp 2026 YLL uses a benchmark age of 75, so SIMAH deaths at 75–79 contribute zero YLL — [Lemp 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamahealthforum.2026.2348); [simulate_mortality.R](https://github.com/charlotteprobst/simah/blob/2fd8c1ea8c95de0a7137e31db2560a5ceb60339d/R/simulate_mortality.R)
- CASCADEPOP: ages 12–80; "Mortality rates before age 80" from CDC databases; census base-population age bands end at 60–80 — [Brennan et al. 2020, DOI](https://doi.org/10.34196/ijm.00217)
- STAPM: age bands to 75–89 in code; STPM single years 11–89 with a new youngest cohort each year (search extraction) — [tobalcepi AlcBinge_stapm.R](https://github.com/stapm-platform/tobalcepi/blob/054ca37190ad901aa5028bd6222e831083472eaa/R/AlcBinge_stapm.R); [STPM page](https://stapm.gitlab.io/STPM.html)
- STAPM ages individuals along subgroup age patterns of consumption — [Holmes et al. 2026, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1004792)
- SAPM v4: oldest price-model band 55+; Italy SBI adaptation reports a 75+ band and results for "65 years or older" — [Addiction 2021, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/add.15464); [BMC Fam Pract 2014, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2296-15-26)
- SPHeP-NCDs: "synthetic life histories (i.e. from birth to death)"; in the cancer analysis most avoided cases occurred at 50–79 (29.5% at 50–59, 29.6% at 60–69, 22.9% at 70–79) — [Cheatley 2021, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/1878-0261.12812)
- Chile ENPG (SENDA): 12–65 years; the age limit was extended to 65 for international comparability, but national trends use 12–64; 2024 wave n=18,668 in 109 comunas; 2014/2016 waves covered urban areas of comunas ≥30,000 inhabitants (~70% of the population) (via search extraction) — [ENPG 2024 press sheet](https://psiconecta.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/ENPG-2024-Prensa.pdf); [SENDA ENPG 2016 report](https://www.senda.gob.cl/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/InformeENPG2016.pdf); [Drogas 2012](https://www.redatam.org/redchl/injuv/injumap/InjuMapIV/docs/fuentes/Drogas_2012.pdf)

### Inferences
- Options for Chile, each mirroring a precedent: (a) cap the simulated population at 65 or 79 and report mortality/YLL only for covered ages (SIMAH/CASCADEPOP precedent of a hard cap); (b) carry ENPG 65-year-olds forward and let them age with a pooled "65+" transition/age effect (SIMAH pools 65+ in one transition group) — requires an assumption that the 65+ age effect is estimable from the 60–65 tail; (c) borrow the age pattern above 65 from another Chilean survey covering older adults, or from GBD/IHME age-specific exposure (SPHeP-NCDs precedent of using IHME exposure by age); (d) STAPM-style ageing along external subgroup age patterns. Which Chilean survey to use for (c) was not researched here.
- Because SIMAH mortality uses TMREL base rates by 10-year age band and education, any cap choice also determines which DEIS age bands must be split into "rate at TMREL" vs observed counts.

### Gaps
- No primary source found that explicitly justifies the 79/80 upper age in SIMAH or CASCADEPOP, or that describes imputing drinking for ages above a survey's upper limit.
- BRFSS/NSDUH/HSE age coverage is stated here from general knowledge (no upper age limit) and was not verified with a source in this session.
