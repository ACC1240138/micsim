# Alcohol screening and brief intervention (SBI / IB) in primary care: evidence and modelling conventions for a Chilean microsimulation module

Scope note: notes compiled 2026-10-09. Most primary articles were read through PubMed/PMC (abstracts, plus full text where PMC had it: Lemp 2026, Manthey 2021, Angus 2014, Barticevic 2021, SCALA/Anderson 2021). The egress proxy blocked direct access to oecd.org, dipres.gob.cl, paho.org, eprints and PMC HTML. Findings from those domains come only from search-engine summaries and are marked "(search summary, not opened)". Unit conversions and parameter proposals are labelled as inferences.

## 1. Effect of BI on consumption, on women, on HED and on drinking above limits

### Takeaway
The anchor effect is Kaner 2018 (Cochrane): about −20 g/week at 12 months (95% CI −28 to −12) versus minimal or no intervention, from a trial mean baseline of 244 g/week. That is roughly −8% relative (inference), with I² = 73%. Effects in women are now considered similar to men. Effects on heavy episodic drinking (HED) are small: −0.08 binges/week. Pooled US reviews give about 12 percentage points (pp) fewer people with heavy-drinking episodes and 11 pp more people below limits. Very brief (<1 min) or technician-delivered single-contact advice has failed in recent trials in Japan and Chile.

### Cited Findings
**Cochrane / Kaner 2018 (BI vs minimal or no intervention; general practice and emergency care; brief = ≤5 sessions and <60 min in total)**
- Review size: 69 RCTs, 33,642 participants. The primary meta-analysis had 34 trials (n = 15,197). Brief intervention reduced consumption at 1 year by MD −20 g/week (95% CI −28 to −12), moderate-quality evidence, with substantial heterogeneity (I² = 73%). Mean baseline consumption in trials that reported it was 244 g/week (30.5 UK units). — [Kaner et al. 2018, Cochrane CD004148.pub4](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD004148.pub4)
- "A subgroup analysis by gender demonstrated that both men and women reduced alcohol consumption after receiving a brief intervention." Sex-specific g/week values are not in the abstract. — [Kaner 2018](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD004148.pub4); the distillation says "Subgroup analysis showed a similar effect for men and women" — [Beyer et al. 2019, Alcohol Alcohol 54(4):417-427](https://doi.org/10.1093/alcalc/agz035)
- HED and pattern outcomes (BI vs control):
  - binges/week: MD −0.08 (95% CI −0.14 to −0.02; 15 studies, n = 6,946)
  - drinking days/week: −0.13 (−0.23 to −0.04; 11 studies)
  - drinking intensity: −0.2 g/drinking day (−3.1 to 2.7; 10 studies)
  - Source: [Kaner 2018](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD004148.pub4)
- Extended (longer) counselling was no better than brief: MD 2 g/week (−42 to 45; 3 studies). Against no or minimal intervention, extended counselling gave −14 g/week (−37 to 9; 6 studies) in the PubMed abstract. The PMC copy of the abstract reads −20 g/week (−40 to 1) for the same comparison, a version discrepancy that is unresolved. — [Kaner 2018](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD004148.pub4)
- Adverse effects: only 5 studies reported them. One study found BI increased binge drinking among women. — [Kaner 2018](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD004148.pub4)
- Plain-language framing: the reduction was "around a pint of beer (475 mL) or a third of a bottle of wine (250 mL) less each week". — [Kaner 2018](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD004148.pub4)

**Sex-specific effects as used by modellers**
- Manthey et al. recalculated Kaner 2018 into proportional reductions in daily drinking: women −15.8% (−31.1% to −1.1%), men −12.0% (−18.6% to −5.7%). — [Manthey et al. 2021, PLOS ONE 16(8):e0255843](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0255843)
- The earlier Cochrane review (Kaner 2007) found benefit in men only: MD −57 g/week (95% CI −89 to −25). For women the MD was −10 g/week, not significant (secondary summary). — [AAFP Cochrane for Clinicians 2009](https://www.aafp.org/afp/2009/0301/p370.html)
- The OECD 2021 SPHeP-NCDs counselling module assumes male drinkers cut by 42.21 g/week and female drinkers by 30.27 g/week (search summary, not opened). — [OECD 2021, Preventing Harmful Alcohol Use (PDF)](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2021/05/preventing-harmful-alcohol-use_0d828f91/6e4b4ffb-en.pdf)

**USPSTF reviews (counselling after screening in primary care; follow-up 6–12 months)**
- Jonas 2012 (23 trials; dependence mostly excluded):
  - consumption fell by 3.6 drinks/week (WMD; 95% CI 2.4 to 4.8; 10 trials; n = 4,332)
  - 12% fewer adults reported heavy drinking episodes (RD 0.12; 0.07 to 0.16; 7 trials; n = 2,737)
  - 11% more adults drank below recommended limits (RD 0.11; 0.08 to 0.13; 9 trials; n = 5,973), over 12 months versus control
  - The best evidence was for brief multicontact interventions of 10–15 minutes.
  - Source: [Jonas et al. 2012, Ann Intern Med 157:645-54](https://doi.org/10.7326/0003-4819-157-9-201211060-00544)
- O'Connor 2018 update (68 studies, n = 36,528):
  - drinks/week WMD −1.6 (−2.2 to −1.0; 32 studies)
  - OR 0.60 (0.53–0.67) for exceeding recommended limits
  - OR 0.67 (0.58–0.77) for reporting a heavy-use episode, at 6–12 months
  - Brief screening instruments had sensitivity and specificity between 0.70 and 0.85 in adults.
  - Source: [O'Connor et al. 2018, JAMA 320:1910-28](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2018.12086)

**Other meta-analyses**
- Platt 2016 (52 trials, n = 29,891, all settings):
  - Brief alcohol interventions (ABIs) reduced quantity consumed by 0.15 SD.
  - Nurse-delivered ABIs gave d = −0.23 (−0.33 to −0.13); brief advice content gave d = −0.20 (−0.30 to −0.09).
  - Setting and content did not significantly moderate effects.
  - Effects were maintained at first and last assessment.
  - Source: [Platt et al. 2016, BMJ Open 6:e011473](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2016-011473)
- Tanner-Smith & Lipsey 2015 (185 samples; adolescents 11–18 and young adults 19–30):
  - Consumption g = 0.27 (adolescents) and g = 0.17 (young adults); alcohol problems g = 0.19 and 0.11.
  - Effects persisted up to 1 year and did not vary by demographics, length or format.
  - Source: [Tanner-Smith & Lipsey 2015, J Subst Abuse Treat 51:1-18](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsat.2014.09.001)

**Recent null pragmatic trials (important for transportability)**
- So et al. 2025, BMJ (pragmatic cluster RCT, Japan):
  - Design: 40 clinics, 1,133 outpatients aged 20–74 with AUDIT-C ≥5 (men) or ≥4 (women). A doctor-delivered "ultra-brief" intervention (<1 min oral advice plus leaflet) was compared with AUDIT-C assessment only.
  - At 24 weeks the difference was +27.8 g/4 weeks (95% CI −149.7 to 205.4), Hedges g 0.02. At 12 weeks it was +54.9 g/4 weeks (−104.1 to 213.9).
  - Both arms drank about 1,000 g/4 weeks at follow-up.
  - Source: [So et al. 2025, BMJ 390:e083985](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2024-083985); accompanying editorial: [Phillips & Drummond 2025, BMJ 390:r1689](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.r1689)
- Chilean trials (details in Q5): a 5-min technician-delivered BI was not superior to a leaflet on AUDIT risk category, with only 0.86 AUDIT points of benefit. An ASSIST-linked BI was not superior to a pamphlet. — [Barticevic et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13722-021-00248-4); [Poblete et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1111/add.13808)

### Inferences
- Unit conversions:
  - Kaner's −20 g/week is −2.86 g/day, which is exactly the mean SIMAH used (Q3).
  - Relative to the 244 g/week trial baseline, it is about −8.2%.
  - Jonas's −3.6 US drinks/week (14 g each) is about −50 g/week. O'Connor's −1.6 drinks/week is about −22 g/week.
  - Absolute effects are therefore 20–50 g/week, and relative effects 8–16%, depending on source and era.
- Older and larger effects come from Kaner 2007 (−38 g/week; 12.3% as used in SAPM) and from Jonas 2012. Newer, more pragmatic evidence is smaller: Kaner 2018 and O'Connor 2018, plus null ultra-brief and technician-delivered trials. Use Kaner 2018 as the base case and the older estimates as an optimistic sensitivity analysis.
- Women: current evidence supports applying the same relative effect by sex, or a slightly larger one (Manthey −15.8% vs −12.0%, wide CI). Do not reuse the 2007 men-only result.
- HED: model it as a separate probability of leaving HED status rather than a consequence of the g/day change. Candidate parameters:
  - RD −0.07 (Kaner-based, as used in SIMAH and Manthey)
  - RD −0.12 (Jonas, optimistic)
  - OR 0.67 (O'Connor)

### Gaps
- Exact Kaner 2018 sex-subgroup estimates in g/week (MD, CI, k) could not be verified: the Cochrane full text was not accessible, and the PMC record holds the abstract only. Manthey's recalculated percentages are the best verified proxy.
- The source of the RD −0.07 for HED (described as Kaner-based "pooled risk difference") could not be checked against the review's forest plot.
- No Latin American meta-analytic estimate in g/week exists. Chilean trials report AUDIT scores, not grams.

## 2. Durability beyond 12 months and decay assumptions used in models

### Takeaway
Trial evidence beyond 12 months is thin and conflicting. Project TrEAT (US, 2 physician visits plus 2 nurse calls) maintained effects to 48 months. The WHO Phase II Australian cohort showed no difference at 10 years. Models therefore use explicit decay assumptions:
- SAPM: linear rebound to baseline over 7 years (pessimistic: 3 years)
- Manthey: full effect 4 years, then linear decline to 0 at year 10 (sensitivity: 0 after 3 years)
- OECD 2015: waning within 12 months
- Lemp/SIMAH: annual re-sampling; decay rule not reported in the main text

### Cited Findings
- Fleming 2002 (Project TrEAT RCT; n = 774; 482 men, 292 women; aged 18–65):
  - Intervention: two physician visits plus two nurse follow-up calls.
  - Significant reductions in 7-day alcohol use, binge episodes and excessive drinking appeared "within 6 months of the intervention and [were] maintained over the 48-month follow-up period".
  - Source: [Fleming et al. 2002, Alcohol Clin Exp Res 26:36-43 (PMID 11821652)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11821652/)
- Wutzke 2002 (WHO Phase II Australian arm; n = 554 non-dependent hazardous/harmful drinkers; interventions of 5–60 min vs no treatment):
  - Lower consumption at 9 months.
  - At 10 years there were no differences in median consumption, reduction from baseline, mortality or ICD-10 dependence/harmful use.
  - Authors: brief advice "without regular follow-up and reinforcement" did not sustain effects at 10 years.
  - Source: [Wutzke et al. 2002, Addiction 97:665-75](https://doi.org/10.1046/j.1360-0443.2002.00080.x)
- The Cochrane primary outcome is fixed at 1 year. Its authors note evidence gaps on the optimum length and frequency needed to sustain longer-term effects (search summary). — [Kaner 2018](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD004148.pub4)
- Effects persisted up to 1 year in young people. — [Tanner-Smith & Lipsey 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsat.2014.09.001)
- Effects were maintained at first and last assessment (follow-up range not given in abstract). — [Platt 2016](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2016-011473)
- A 2026 systematic review of 32 RCTs of brief motivational interviewing found effects "frequently attenuated beyond 6–12 months", suggesting booster contacts. — [Ho et al. 2026, Addict Behav Rep 24:100727](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.abrep.2026.100727)

**Model decay conventions (exact parameters)**
- SAPM, Italy adaptation (same model family as England):
  - A 12.3% consumption reduction after a 24.9-min intervention (from Kaner 2007 Cochrane) is applied to every BI recipient.
  - This "reduction is assumed to rebound linearly towards the patient's baseline consumption over a period of 7 years in line with the findings of Fleming et al."
  - Pessimistic sensitivity analyses: a 5.9% reduction, a "3 year rebound", and both combined.
  - Source: [Angus et al. 2014, BMC Fam Pract 15:26](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2296-15-26)
- Manthey 2021, Germany (open-source R model):
  - Effects on drinking level are "stable for a period of four years … attenuate thereafter, and nullify after ten years (linearly imputed for all years five to ten)".
  - For people who stop HED after BI, the "chance to re-engage in HED was 50% … starting from the second year post intervention".
  - Sensitivity: all effects vanish 3 years after intervention. This raised 2018 per-capita consumption by 5.2% (50% screening scenario) and 6.8% (75% scenario) relative to the main analysis.
  - Source: [Manthey et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0255843)
- OECD 2015 (as reported by Manthey): screening coverage 40%, 30% intervention probability among positives, "with the effects waning within 12 months after receiving the intervention". Over 40 years this reduced hazardous/harmful drinking prevalence by 5%, with no measurable HED effect. — [Manthey et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0255843)
- WHO-CHOICE/OneHealth (Chisholm 2018; secondary source):
  - Brief psychosocial treatment costs were applied to 50% of prevalent hazardous/harmful cases in year 1 and every fifth year thereafter. Half of incident cases were covered in other years, "to reflect a finite treatment effect".
  - 3 visits per case; 20% referred to outpatient care and 5% to hospital.
  - Sources: [PAHO SCALA slides, Rehm 2021 (search summary, not opened)](https://paho.org/sites/default/files/2021-06/SCALA-JRehm_SCALA_global-context-28June21.pdf); cost-effectiveness ratio <I$150 (low-income) and <I$1,500 (high-income) per healthy life year — [Chisholm et al. 2018, J Stud Alcohol Drugs 79:514-522 (PMID 30079865)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30079865/)
- Lemp 2026 (SIMAH): individuals are sampled through the cascade independently each year "allowing individuals to be resampled in any year". The main text gives no decay function; details are in eMethods 1.3.5. — [Lemp et al. 2026, JAMA Health Forum 7(7):e262348](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamahealthforum.2026.2348)
- OECD 2021: duration and coverage assumptions of the counselling scenario could not be retrieved (Q3 Gaps).

### Inferences
- A defensible Chilean base case is the SAPM convention: full relative effect in year 1, then linear rebound to 0 over 7 years. Two alternatives bracket it:
  - Optimistic: Manthey's plateau of 4 years, then linear to 0 at year 10.
  - Pessimistic: linear to 0 over 3 years, or the OECD-2015 style 1-year effect.
- Decay for HED should be faster than for volume. Manthey's 50% re-engagement from year 2 is a ready-made parameter.
- The decay assumption matters most at high coverage. Manthey's sensitivity changed results by 5–7% of per-capita consumption only in the 50–75% screening scenarios. At current Chilean coverage (~8%) it will barely matter.

### Gaps
- No meta-analysis pooling outcomes beyond 12 months in primary-care BI was found. The long-term evidence rests on single trials (Fleming; Wutzke).
- The Purshouse 2013 England decay and effect parameters were not verified directly. The abstract and the Angus 2014 statement ("broadly comparable") suggest the same SAPM conventions.
- Not verified: the SIMAH (Lemp 2026) eMethods rule for decay and for cumulative versus non-cumulative repeat effects, and the OECD 2021 effect duration.

## 3. How models apply the effect: who, which scale, HED, quitting, repeats

### Takeaway
Most microsimulations apply a relative (proportional) reduction in g/day to screen-positive drinkers who actually receive BI. Lemp/SIMAH samples an absolute g/day effect from the trial distribution and then scales it to individual consumption. HED is changed by a separate Bernoulli draw with RD −0.07. Eligibility is defined either by g/day thresholds (WHO risk categories) or by AUDIT-C (≥5 men / ≥4 women). No reviewed model moves BI recipients into abstention. Repeat screening is either forbidden (SAPM) or allowed each year (Manthey, SIMAH).

### Cited Findings
**Lemp et al. 2026, SIMAH (US; the template the project follows)**
- Structure: annual individual-level microsimulation of adults 18–79 from 2000 to 2030. The ASBI cascade (routine health-care access, then screening, then BI for hazardous drinkers) comes from regression models fitted to NSDUH 2013–2019.
- Eligibility for BI is hazardous use: >20 g/day for women and >40 g/day for men (WHO categories). A sensitivity analysis adds category I (≤20/≤40 g/day) and "had very little impact".
- Only *newly* intervened individuals get effects, "because the effects of existing interventions are assumed to be already captured in historical alcohol use data".
- Each newly intervened individual gets a GPD reduction sampled from the trial-level effect distribution (mean [SE] −2.86 [0.58] g/day), "scaled using each individual's current level of consumption". Sensitivity means are −1.71 and −4.01 g/day.
- HED: "Among newly intervened individuals classified as heavy episodic drinkers, a proportion corresponding to the pooled risk difference (RD, −0.07; 95% CI −0.02 to −0.12) were randomly selected to transition to non-HED status". The GPD and HED draws are independent.
- HED is defined as ≥60 g on at least 1 occasion in the past 30 days and modifies the injury risk functions.
- Outcomes are YLL before age 75 for AUD, liver disease (including HCV-cirrhosis with an attenuated RR), motor-vehicle injuries, other unintentional injuries and suicide. Cancers were excluded because of latency.
- Source for all of the above: [Lemp et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamahealthforum.2026.2348)

**Manthey et al. 2021 (Germany; open-access R code in supplement)**
- Cascade conditions: attend PHC, then be screened, then drink riskily, then receive BI (medium risk) or referral to treatment (high risk). Referral is assumed to have the same effect as BI.
- Risk bands:
  - Medium: women 21–40 g/day, men 41–60 g/day.
  - High: women ≥41 g/day, men ≥61 g/day.
- They "applied the proportional reduction of drinking levels from baseline to account for … under-reporting" because consumption was calibrated to sales-based per-capita consumption (APC). HED status was changed using the RD −0.07.
- Annual cycle: decay of previous effects and secular-trend correction first, then the new year's SBI effects. One illustrated individual received three interventions in 10 years.
- Source: [Manthey et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0255843)

**SAPM (Purshouse 2013 England; Angus 2014 Italy)**
- Screening uses AUDIT-C ≥5 (men) / ≥4 (women). The probability of screening positive given consumption came from UK Psychiatric Morbidity Surveys 2000 and 2007. A 10-min BI is given to all screen-positives, with a 12.3% relative reduction.
- "Each individual … can be screened no more than once." No effect is assumed for people screened without BI.
- Health effects come through potential impact fractions (PIFs) with time lags for chronic conditions. Acute conditions depend on HED frequency (>6 glasses) with linear risk.
- Programme: 10 years of SBI with a 30-year horizon.
- Sources: [Angus et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2296-15-26); [Purshouse et al. 2013, Alcohol Alcohol 48:180-8](https://doi.org/10.1093/alcalc/ags103)

**OECD 2021**
- The counselling module detects at-risk patients at a GP visit and targets hazardous and harmful drinkers (regular or episodic), "excluding individuals dependent on alcohol, aged 18-70". Effects are in g/week (men −42.21, women −30.27) (search summary, not opened). — [OECD 2021](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2021/05/preventing-harmful-alcohol-use_0d828f91/6e4b4ffb-en.pdf)

**SIMAH policy-effect sequence (price module, same team)**
- Price effects were applied as individual-level percent changes via beverage-specific elasticities. This is the SIMAH convention for proportional changes in g/day. — [Kilian et al. 2025, Lancet Public Health 10:e815-23](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-2667(25)00165-3)

### Inferences
- Recommended Chilean implementation (consistent with SIMAH and the AGENTS.md policy-effect sequence):
  1. Annual cascade draw: PHC contact (FONASA-enrolled, by sex, age and education), screening (EMPA/DIR AUDIT), screen-positive status (AUDIT 8–15, or a g/day proxy), BI received.
  2. For incremental BI only: multiply g/day by (1 + r), where r is drawn from the Kaner-based relative effect (about −8% base; −12% men / −16% women Manthey-style alternative; −12.3% SAPM optimistic).
  3. Separate Bernoulli draw for HED to non-HED with p = 0.07.
  4. Apply decay to the effect of each past BI.
  5. Recode categories, then compute PIF and RR, then deaths and YLL.
- Use a relative effect rather than SIMAH's absolute −2.86 g/day if Chilean g/day is rescaled to per-capita consumption (as Manthey did). An absolute trial effect applied to under-reported survey grams, or to rescaled grams, would be mis-scaled.
- Do not allow BI to create abstainers, since no reference model does. Optionally route AUDIT ≥16/≥20 to referral with the same or zero effect, flagged as an assumption (Manthey's equal-effect assumption is explicitly conservative and untested).
- Repeat handling: either SAPM's "once per lifetime" or annual eligibility with a non-additive rule. Example of a non-additive rule: a new BI resets the decay clock instead of compounding. Compounding repeated −12% reductions has no trial support.

### Gaps
- SIMAH's exact scaling formula ("scaled using each individual's current level of consumption") and its repeat/decay rule are in eMethods, which could not be accessed.
- No model was found that varies the BI effect by baseline drinking level, SES or age. Lemp states evidence was insufficient for subgroup effects.

## 4. Delivery cascade parameters (contact, screening, positivity, delivery, repetition)

### Takeaway
Real-world cascades are leaky. Screening coverage ranges widely:
- Germany: 2.9% of PHC patients
- ODHIN Europe baseline: 5.9%
- SCALA Latin America: about 1–2% of registered adults in 5 months without training
- US NSDUH-calibrated: 56% of past-year drinkers
- UK: 49% recorded in 5 years
- US Veterans Health Administration (VA): 93%

BI delivery among screen-positives also varies: about 50% (Germany assumption), "just under 80%" (ODHIN), and only about 1 in 5 US risky drinkers advised. Modelled programmes reach 13–49% of adults with BI over 10 years. Population effects become measurable only at screening coverage of about 25% or more.

### Cited Findings
**US (SIMAH)**
- 15% of US adults had no health-care visit in the past 12 months. About 4 in 5 attending a "health checkup" were asked about alcohol, but only 1 in 5 risky drinkers received advice to cut down.
- Reference scenario: the mean screened share of past-year drinkers is 55.7%. By 2030, 18.0% of hazardous-drinking men and 12.2% of hazardous-drinking women receive BI annually.
- Expansion scenarios:
  - +20 M screens raises the screened share to 66.9%. Screening-only expansion gave YLL reductions indistinguishable from zero.
  - +8 M BI (3.6× reference BI volume) reduced YLL by −51.3 per 100k in men and −34.1 per 100k in women by 2030.
- An AHRQ initiative raised screening from 23.2% to 35.8% and brief counselling from 20.1% to 27.6%.
- Source for all of the above: [Lemp et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamahealthforum.2026.2348)

**Germany (Manthey 2021)**
- Inputs:
  - PHC visit probability by sex, age, education and drinking level (2016 survey).
  - Screening probability 2.9% (Bremen survey), varied over 0/10/25/50/75%.
  - BI or referral delivered to 50% of screen-positives (GP survey).
- Coverage results:
  - At 2.9%, 21.0% of adults were screened at least once in 10 years and 2.5% received an intervention.
  - At 10% screening, intervention reach was 7.6%; at 50%, 21.1% (99.1% ever screened); at 75%, 23.7%.
- Consumption effects at 10 years versus baseline:
  - No significant change at ≤10% screening.
  - 50% screening: −11.4% (−15.2 to −4.4); 75% screening: −17.0%.
  - HED did not change significantly in any scenario.
- Other benchmarks cited in the same paper: UK 48.8% of adult records had alcohol recorded in the previous 5 years (2018); US VA 93%.
- Source: [Manthey et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0255843)

**Europe (ODHIN, SAPM)**
- ODHIN (120 PHC units in 5 countries; 12-week implementation): the baseline proportion of consulting adults screened was 0.059. Ratios versus no strategy:
  - Training and support: 1.48 (1.13–1.95)
  - Financial reimbursement: 2.00 (1.56–2.56)
  - Both: 2.34 (1.77–3.10)
  - eBI referral did not increase screening.
  - Source: [Anderson et al. 2016, Addiction 111:1935-45](https://doi.org/10.1111/add.13476)
- At 9 months only training and support had a lasting effect on intervention delivery (ratio 1.39; 1.03–1.88). — [Anderson et al. 2017, Ann Fam Med 15:335-40](https://doi.org/10.1370/afm.2051)
- In ODHIN, advice was given to "just under 80%" of patients with high measured consumption (as reported by the SCALA authors). — [Anderson et al. 2021, J Gen Intern Med 36:2663-71](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11606-020-06503-9)
- Cost-effectiveness of ODHIN strategies was modelled with SAPM over 30 years in England, Poland and the Netherlands. Results were "sensitive to … alternative BI effect assumptions" (parameter values not seen). — [Angus et al. 2019, Eur J Public Health 29:219-25](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurpub/cky181)
- SAPM coverage over a 10-year programme:
  - Italy, screening at next GP registration: 63% screened, 32% receive BI.
  - Italy, screening at next consultation: 97% screened, 49% BI (84% screened in year 1).
  - England, registration approach: 39% screened, 13% BI.
  - Source: [Angus et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2296-15-26)
- Purshouse 2013 England: registration captures about 40% over 10 years; consultation captures 96% but needs heavy first-year resourcing. — [Purshouse et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1093/alcalc/ags103)
- The EU-wide meta-model of 28 countries uses the proportion of the population screened over 10 years as a key driver. — [Angus et al. 2017, Eur J Public Health 27:345-51](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurpub/ckw122)

**International coverage assumptions**
- OECD 2015: 40% screening coverage, 30% intervention probability among positives (per Manthey). — [Manthey et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0255843)
- An IAS summary of OECD 2021 says fewer than 10% of people with harmful drinking receive primary-care support. — [Institute of Alcohol Studies 2021](https://www.ias.org.uk/2021/09/28/preventing-harmful-alcohol-consumption-is-an-excellent-investment-for-countries/)
- WHO-CHOICE: 50% of prevalent cases in year 1, re-treated every 5th year (secondary). — [PAHO slides (search summary, not opened)](https://paho.org/sites/default/files/2021-06/SCALA-JRehm_SCALA_global-context-28June21.pdf)

**Latin America (SCALA; Bogotá, Mexico City, Lima; 58 PHC centres; AUDIT-C based)**
- Baseline coverage was 2.1 per 1,000 registered adults measured in 1 month; the country investigators considered routine activity non-existent. After 5 months the cumulative figure was 11.7 per 1,000.
- Trained centres reached 14.1 per 1,000 versus 1.1 per 1,000 untrained (IRR 9.8; 4.1–24.7). Municipal support added nothing (IRR 1.0; 0.6–1.8 in the full text; the abstract's "0.6 to 0.8" is a typo). A shorter package was non-inferior (IRR 0.8; 0.4–1.5).
- 72.3% of providers attended at least one training session.
- The protocol powered for 3.25% of registered adults measured at 12 months in control, 7.5% with training and 15% with training plus municipal support. COVID-19 cut the planned 18-month implementation.
- Source: [Anderson et al. 2021, J Gen Intern Med](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11606-020-06503-9)
- Over the whole implementation, providers screened over 20,000 patients and advised almost 1,000 heavy drinkers (thesis summary; no denominator). — [Maastricht thesis (search summary, not opened)](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/files/133148423/s7840.pdf)
- Barriers rated highest in Colombia, Mexico and Peru were patients' normalised perception of heavy drinking, lack of ongoing provider support, poor access to referral services and lenient alcohol control laws. — [Kokole et al. 2021, Prim Health Care Res Dev 22:e4](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1463423620000675)

### Inferences
- A Chilean cascade should be parameterised as four annual probabilities:
  1. PHC contact (FONASA enrolment × visit), using sex, age and education gradients
  2. Screening given contact
  3. Positivity given true drinking state
  4. BI given positivity
- Benchmarks: Chilean screening reaches about 8% of the population (Q5). Delivery among positives is about 6% when computed as BI/screened (Q5), with 11% positivity in one check-up sample.
- Screening-only expansion is not worth simulating as a main scenario (null in SIMAH). Scenarios should vary BI volume.
- SCALA-type coverage (≈1–2% of registered adults per 5 months) is far below the ≥25% annual screening that Manthey found necessary for detectable population effects.

### Gaps
- No verified "SCALA 2021 Lancet Regional Health – Americas" paper was found. PubMed and web searches returned only the JGIM 5-month results, the protocol and process papers. The 18-month or final outcomes (proportion screened positive and proportion advised by country) could not be verified.
- The OECD 2021 counselling scenario's coverage/uptake parameters could not be retrieved (OECD site blocked).

## 5. Chile and Latin America specifics (trials, programmes, coverage, usual care)

### Takeaway
Chile has had a national public-sector SBIRT programme in primary care since 2011 (now "DIR", formalised in 2019). It uses the AUDIT (BI for scores 8–15) inside the preventive check-up (EMPA). In 2018 it screened about 1.27 M people and delivered about 76 k BIs, about 8% population coverage. Both Chilean RCTs (Barticevic 2021; Poblete 2017) found BI not superior to a leaflet or pamphlet, the programme's own minimal "usual care", although both arms improved strongly. Only about 30% of Chileans report ever being asked about alcohol by a health professional.

### Cited Findings
**Barticevic et al. 2021 RCT (Addict Sci Clin Pract 16:39; NCT02642757)**
- Setting: five public PHC centres in Santiago (San Miguel, Puente Alto, La Pintana), March 2016 to July 2017.
- Population and screening:
  - Patients were identified during the "preventive annual health assessment, which routinely includes the AUDIT" (EMPA).
  - Of 3,247 AUDITs, 11% (357) scored 8–15 (intermediate risk), and 342 were randomized.
  - Participants: mean age 29, 57% male, mean AUDIT 10.5.
  - People with AUDIT >15 received BI plus referral for medical evaluation and were excluded.
- Arms:
  - Health-technician (HT) 5-min BI plus leaflet (n = 174): normative feedback, health information, change plan.
  - Leaflet alone (n = 168; <1 min): limits, planner, strategies.
  - The leaflet was chosen because it "represents the minimal level of intervention within the Chilean SBIRT program", and ethics committees required it.
- Results at 6 months (86% follow-up):
  - Moved to low risk: 80% (BI) vs 71% (leaflet); aOR 0.6 (0.34–1.05), not significant.
  - AUDIT fell by 5.76 vs 5.07 points; adjusted difference 0.86 (0.06–1.66), Cohen's d 0.21. AUDIT-C difference 0.44 (0–0.88).
- Programme context reported in the paper:
  - The SBIRT programme was created in 2011 in the public PHC network, "which serves around 80% of the Chilean population".
  - "In 2018, 1,268,640 people were screened, and 75,854 received a BI in the public health care sector … population coverage of approximately 8%". Private-sector delivery is not widespread.
  - BIs were initially delivered by doctors, nurses and midwives, then shifted to HTs because of shortages.
- National low-risk limits in the leaflet: ≤3 drinks/occasion for women and ≤4 for men; ≤7/week for women and ≤14/week for men.
- Source: [Barticevic et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13722-021-00248-4)

**Poblete et al. 2017 RCT (Addiction 112:1462-9)**
- Design: ASSIST-linked BI for alcohol and drugs within a systematic screening programme (19 PHC centres, 8 emergency rooms, 5 police stations). 12,217 people aged 19–55 were screened and 806 randomized. ASSIST alcohol scores 11–20 (moderate risk).
- Arms: BI (n = 400) vs informational pamphlet (n = 406).
- Results at 3 months (62% follow-up): no difference in ASSIST alcohol score (MD 0.18; −1.45 to 1.10).
- Source: [Poblete et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1111/add.13808)

**Other Chilean sources**
- A Chilean exploratory RCT protocol tests brief motivational treatment (4 × 45-min sessions by a psychologist) versus enhanced usual care for alcohol use disorder in PHC. Results were not found. — [Barticevic et al. 2020, Trials 21:692](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13063-020-04589-4)
- DIR programme (Programa de Detección, Intervención y Referencia Asistida para alcohol, tabaco y otras drogas; search summary of DIPRES documents, not opened):
  - Approved by Resolución Exenta N° 992 of 19 December 2019.
  - Screens people aged ≥10 enrolled in health centres (FONASA beneficiaries) and assigns the intervention by risk pattern.
  - Reach is described as about 8% of each comuna's potential population, set by budget.
  - People detected with risky use: 13,555 (2012) and 77,294 (2019).
  - Component spending was M$2,779,831 in 2021 (in 2022 pesos).
  - Sources: [DIPRES ex-ante evaluation](https://www.dipres.gob.cl/597/articles-275874_doc_pdf.pdf); [DIPRES monitoring sheet](https://www.dipres.gob.cl/597/articles-212526_doc_pdf.pdf)
- ENS 2016–2017: 11.7% of the population aged ≥15 had a risky drinking pattern (search summary of MINSAL press release). — [MINSAL](https://www.minsal.cl/encuesta-minsal-revela-alto-nivel-de-consumo-de-alcohol-en-chile/)
- Asked about alcohol by a health professional (ENS 2017; n = 5,240; search summary of abstract): about 30% overall (95% CI 27.8–32.5). Men 31.6%, women 28.8%; lowest at ages 15–24 (25.1%), highest at 45–64 (33%). — [Durán-Santa Cruz et al. 2024, Ars Medica 49(3)](https://doi.org/10.11565/arsmed.v49i3.2007)
- The EMPA in Chilean PHC "rinde menos de lo esperado", partly because of high loss of contact after the first screening encounter. A 7-centre pilot ran in 2023 (search summary). — [Boletín Academia Chilena de Medicina 2025](https://mail.bolacadmed.cl/index.php/bacm/article/download/150/258/407)
- Private sector: Isapre preventive plans list a "Bebedor Problema" activity (AUDIT plus counselling) once a year for people ≥15 (search summary; exact source document uncertain). — [Banmédica PPI Adulto](https://banmedica.cl/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/PPI_3_Adulto-BM.pdf)

**Latin America**
- In SCALA, PHC-based alcohol measurement and advice was "not normally undertaken" in Colombia, Mexico or Peru before the study; numbers are in Q4. — [Anderson et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11606-020-06503-9)
- Over 70% of Latin American countries report limited or scarce access to health services for alcohol (as cited by Barticevic). — [Barticevic et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13722-021-00248-4)

### Inferences
- Chilean cascade benchmarks (2018):
  - BI/screened = 75,854 / 1,268,640 ≈ 6.0%, which is close to the 11% AUDIT 8–15 positivity seen at check-ups if about half of positives get BI.
  - Screened/population is about 8% per year (MINSAL figure).
  - These are candidate baseline (status-quo) parameters. The existing programme's effect is already embedded in observed ENPG drinking. Following SIMAH, apply effects only to *incremental* BI in counterfactual scenarios.
- Usual-care comparator in Chile is not "no intervention": the leaflet or pamphlet is the programme minimum, and both Chilean trials compared BI against it. The effective Chilean contrast for BI is plausibly smaller than Kaner's BI vs minimal/no intervention. This argues for the Kaner-based base effect only when modelling expansion from no screening, and a reduced effect (e.g., 50%, or the null from the Chilean trials) as sensitivity when modelling better BI versus leaflet.
- Delivery by health technicians in 5 min, together with null ultra-brief results (Japan), suggests a "delivery quality" multiplier below 1 for Chile, flagged as an assumption.
- Transportability limits:
  - Kaner trials are mostly high-income country trials with a baseline of 244 g/week.
  - Chilean BI targets AUDIT 8–15 rather than g/day bands.
  - Chilean outcomes are AUDIT points, not grams.
  - ENPG g/day must be mapped to AUDIT-risk eligibility (or AUDIT-C bands, if ENPG includes AUDIT items; not verified here).

### Gaps
- The DIPRES and MINSAL programme documents could not be opened. Programme figures (DIR cut-offs AUDIT 8–15 / 16–19 / ≥20, annual series of screens and BIs, sex/age breakdown, share delivered by technicians) are unverified beyond Barticevic's 2018 figure and search summaries.
- No Chilean data were found on: EMPA coverage of adults per year, the proportion of screen-positives receiving BI by sex and age, the "Vida Sana" alcohol programme's coverage, or repeat-screening intervals.
- No Chilean or Latin American trial has follow-up beyond 6 months or reports grams. No Latin American BI effect estimate in g/day exists.
- The ENS 2016–2017 AUDIT ≥8 prevalence was not verified by sex. The proportion of patients advised to reduce drinking in ENS 2017 was not retrievable.
